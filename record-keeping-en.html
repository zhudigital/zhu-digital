Business Record Keeping:
What to Keep and How Long

The ATO requires every business to keep accurate financial records. Good record keeping isn't just a legal obligation — it makes tax time easier, supports your deduction claims, and protects you if you're audited. Most records must be kept for at least 5 years.

📅 Data current: October 2026 · ATO 2026–27 🕐 5 min read ✅ Free, no sign-up

Retention Periods

How long must you keep records?

Different records have different minimum retention periods. The ATO can audit up to 5 years back for most businesses, and longer for CGT assets.

Record typeMinimum retentionNotes
General tax records
(income, expenses, BAS, GST)		5 yearsFrom the date prepared, obtained, or the transaction occurred
Capital Gains Tax (CGT) records5 years after disposalKeep from when you acquired the asset, not just when you sold it
Employee & payroll records7 yearsUnder Fair Work Act — includes timesheets, pay slips, super records
Company records (ASIC)7 yearsFinancial statements, director minutes, shareholder records
Super fund choice forms5 yearsFrom the date the employee made the choice
Trust deed & trust recordsIndefinitelyKeep for the life of the trust and beyond
⚠️ Common mistake: Many small business owners discard records after 3 years thinking that's the ATO's limit. The minimum is 5 years for most records. Disposing of records early can result in lost deductions and penalties during an audit.

What to Keep

The essential records every business needs

Records can be kept digitally or on paper. Digital copies are acceptable to the ATO provided they are a true and clear reproduction of the original.

🧾
Sales & income records
All tax invoices you issue to customers. Cash register receipts or point-of-sale records. Bank statements showing payments received. Any contracts for services rendered.
💳
Purchase & expense records
Tax invoices from suppliers (required to claim GST credits). Receipts for business expenses. Bank and credit card statements. Loan documents and interest statements.
👥
Employee records
Employment contracts. Time and wages records (hours worked, rates paid). Payslips issued to employees. Super fund details and choice forms. Leave records.
🏠
Asset records
Purchase invoices for equipment and assets. Depreciation schedules. Records of improvements (especially for property). Records kept until 5 years after you sell or dispose of the asset.
🚗
Vehicle & travel records
Logbook for vehicle use (if claiming car expenses). Receipts for fuel, servicing, registration. Records of business travel (airfares, accommodation, meals where claimable).
🏦
Bank & financial records
All business bank account statements. Loan agreements and repayment schedules. Records of any drawings (for sole traders and partnerships). BAS lodgements and ATO correspondence.
✓ Digital tip: You can use accounting software (Xero, MYOB, QuickBooks), a cloud folder (Google Drive, iCloud, Dropbox), or an app like Dext or AutoEntry to photograph and store receipts. Digital records are fully accepted by the ATO as long as they are legible and complete.
