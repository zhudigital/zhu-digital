Choosing Your Business Structure
Is the Most Important First Step

Before registering an ABN in Australia, you need to decide how you'll operate. The wrong structure means higher tax, personal liability, and headaches when you grow. This guide helps you choose in under 5 minutes.

The Four Structures

Click to explore each structure in full

Pros, cons, costs, and who each one suits — all here.

👤
Sole Trader
The most common starting structure in Australia
You and your business are one and the same. Simplest to set up, cheapest to run — ideal when you're starting small, working alone, and keeping costs low.
Registration Cost
$0
ABN is free. Business name costs $39/yr if you trade under a name other than your own.
Tax Rate
19–47%
Taxed at personal income tax rates plus 2% Medicare Levy.
Personal Liability
Unlimited
Your home, savings, and car can be used to pay business debts.
Complexity
⭐ Easiest
Register at abr.gov.au — about 5 minutes, free.
✓ Advantages
  • Free to register, minimal paperwork
  • Full control — no need to consult others
  • Tax reported in your personal return
  • No minimum turnover required
✕ Disadvantages
  • Unlimited personal liability
  • Harder to raise finance from banks
  • High earners pay more tax than a company
  • Business may cease if you become ill or pass away
Best for: Side projects, freelancers, family micro-businesses, expected annual income under $75,000, and anyone not yet ready to hire staff.
🏢
Proprietary Limited Company
Pty Ltd — a separate legal entity from you personally
The company exists independently. As a shareholder, your losses are capped at what you invested — suitable for owners who want asset protection and plan to grow.
Registration Cost
$636
Paid to ASIC once. Plus $342/year annual review fee.
Tax Rate
25%
Small business company rate (turnover under $50M). Base rate entities pay 30%.
Personal Liability
Limited
Personal assets protected. However, directors have legal duties and can be held personally liable for serious breaches.
Complexity
⭐⭐ Moderate
Requires Director ID, a constitution, registered with ASIC or an agent.
✓ Advantages
  • Limited liability protects personal assets
  • Lower tax rate than high personal income
  • Easier to raise finance or attract investors
  • Company continues regardless of ownership changes
✕ Disadvantages
  • Higher setup and annual compliance costs
  • Must lodge a separate company tax return
  • Dividends paid to yourself still attract personal tax
  • Directors have clear legal obligations and can face fines
Best for: Higher earners (profit over $80K/yr), owners who need asset protection, businesses planning to hire, or those bidding on contracts.
🤝
Partnership
Two or more people running a business together
Profits and losses are split by agreement; each partner pays tax individually. A partnership agreement isn't legally required, but is strongly recommended.
Registration Cost
$0+
Free ABN. A legally drafted partnership agreement costs ~$500–$1,500.
Tax Rate
19–47%
Partnership itself doesn't pay tax. Each partner reports their share in their personal return.
Personal Liability
Joint & Unlimited
Each partner is liable for all partnership debts, including those caused by the other partner.
Complexity
⭐ Simple
Register an ABN for the partnership. Agreement is critical.
✓ Advantages
  • Share resources, skills, and risk
  • Flexible profit-sharing arrangements
  • No double taxation — transparent structure
  • Simpler to set up than a company
✕ Disadvantages
  • One partner's mistake can cost everyone
  • Departure of a partner can dissolve the business
  • Personal assets remain at risk
  • Disputes can be complex and costly
Best for: Couples running a business together, friends co-founding a small venture, or professional pairs (designers, consultants) — provided there's trust and a clear written agreement.
🏛️
Trust
A legal arrangement for holding and distributing assets
A trustee manages assets for the benefit of beneficiaries. Common in family businesses; allows flexible income distribution to members on lower tax rates.
Setup Cost
$500+
Trust deed drafting ~$500–$1,500; plus $636 if you use a corporate trustee.
Tax Rate
Flexible
Distributed to beneficiaries at their personal rates. Undistributed income taxed at 47%.
Personal Liability
Varies
Assets held in trust offer some personal protection, but trustee liability depends on structure type.
Complexity
⭐⭐⭐ Complex
Requires solicitor and accountant; ongoing compliance costs are high.
✓ Advantages
  • Distribute income to lower-tax-rate family members
  • Asset protection from personal creditors
  • Effective for estate planning and wealth succession
  • Access to CGT discount for assets held >12 months
✕ Disadvantages
  • Highest setup and ongoing management costs
  • Losses cannot be distributed to beneficiaries
  • ATO scrutiny of Section 100A arrangements increasing
  • Not suitable for businesses seeking external investment
Best for: Family businesses involving multiple members, those focused on asset protection and estate planning — but only with an experienced accountant.

How to Register an ABN

Ready to register? Here's how

Steps below are for sole trader / partnership (most common). Company registration goes through ASIC and differs slightly.

1
Get your TFN (Tax File Number) ready
You need your personal TFN to apply for an ABN. If you don't have one, apply on the ATO website first — takes up to 28 days. Available to Australian citizens and permanent residents.
→ Apply for a TFN on ATO
2
Apply for your ABN on the ABR website
Go to abr.gov.au and click "Apply for an ABN." Enter your name, TFN, business type, and address. Takes 5–10 minutes, is free, and most applications are approved the same day.
→ Register at abr.gov.au
3
Register a business name (optional)
If you trade under your own name, you don't need to register one. If you want a brand name (e.g. "Zhu Digital"), register it with ASIC for $39/year or $92 for 3 years.
→ Register a business name with ASIC
4
Check if you need to register for GST
If your expected annual turnover exceeds $75,000, you must register for GST at the same time. Once registered, you'll lodge a BAS each quarter. If under the threshold, registration is optional — but compulsory once you cross it (within 21 days).
→ Read the GST & BAS guide
5
Start keeping records from day one
The ATO requires most business records to be kept for at least 5 years from the date they were prepared. This includes invoices, receipts, bank records, and payroll. Build the habit from your very first transaction.
→ Read the record-keeping guide
