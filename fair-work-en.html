Employee vs Contractor

Getting the classification right matters

Misclassifying a worker as a contractor when they are legally an employee is one of the most common — and costly — Fair Work errors small businesses make.

👤 Employee
  • Works set hours you determine
  • Uses your tools and equipment
  • Works exclusively (or mostly) for you
  • Cannot subcontract the work
  • Is paid regularly (weekly/fortnightly)
🧑‍💻 Contractor
  • Sets their own hours and methods
  • Uses their own tools/equipment
  • Works for multiple clients
  • Has their own ABN and invoices you
  • Quotes for specific jobs or projects
⚠️ Warning: Calling someone a "contractor" on paper doesn't make them one. Courts look at the actual working relationship. If the ATO or Fair Work finds you misclassified an employee as a contractor, you may owe unpaid super, entitlements, and face significant penalties.

Payslip Requirements

Every payslip must include these details

Failure to provide proper payslips carries penalties. Issue payslips within one working day of each pay period.

📋
Employer details
Your business name and ABN. The employee's name. The pay period start and end dates.
💰
Pay details
Gross pay amount. Any deductions (e.g. tax withheld, salary sacrifice). Net pay amount. If hourly: rate of pay and hours worked.
🏦
Superannuation
Amount of super contributed (or to be contributed). Name of the super fund. The period the contribution covers.
🕐
Leave balances
Annual leave balance (in hours or days). Personal/carer's leave balance. Not required for casuals, but good practice.
