Overseas Income & Cross-Border Tax

How Australia taxes foreign income, the foreign income tax offset, tax treaty protections, and what to disclose — for residents with overseas earnings and international business activity.

Step 1: Confirm Your Tax Residency

Your tax residency status determines what income Australia can tax. It is not the same as citizenship, PR status, or how long you've lived here.

🏠 Australian Tax Resident Taxed on income from all sources worldwide. You receive the tax-free threshold ($18,200 in 2025–26) and can claim the FITO for foreign taxes paid.
✈️ Foreign Resident for Tax Purposes Taxed only on income with an Australian source (e.g. rent from Australian property, wages from Australian employment). No tax-free threshold. No FITO.

The ATO's Residency Tests

The ATO applies four tests in order. Passing any one makes you an Australian tax resident:

TestKey CriterionNotes
Resides testOrdinary factual residence in AustraliaBased on physical presence, habits, family, business ties — the primary test
Domicile testDomicile in Australia and no permanent place of abode overseasRelevant for those who leave Australia
183-day testPresent in Australia for ≥183 days in the income yearDoes not apply if your usual place of abode is outside Australia
Superannuation testMember of certain Commonwealth super fundsCovers government employees posted overseas
⚠️ Residency is a complex legal determination The ATO looks at all facts and circumstances. If you have recently moved to or from Australia, or split time between countries, seek advice specific to your situation. The ATO's Tax Residency tool on ato.gov.au provides a starting point.

Types of Overseas Income — What to Declare

As an Australian tax resident, you must declare all of the following in your Australian tax return:

Foreign Employment Income

Wages, salary, bonuses, or contractor payments received from an overseas employer — even if the money stays in an overseas bank account.

  • Convert to AUD at the exchange rate when received (or ATO annual average)
  • Claim FITO for income tax withheld by the overseas employer
  • Some income may be exempt under a tax treaty (seek advice)
  • Allowances and fringe benefits from foreign employers are also assessable

Foreign Investment Income

Dividends, interest, rent, and capital gains from assets held overseas are all assessable in Australia.

  • Dividends: Gross dividend (before foreign withholding tax) is assessable; claim FITO for the withholding tax
  • Interest: Full amount assessable; claim FITO for any withholding tax deducted
  • Foreign rental property: Declare rent; claim allowable deductions (loan interest, rates, depreciation)
  • Capital gains: CGT applies to foreign property sold after you became a resident; 50% discount may apply for assets held 12+ months

Foreign Business Income

Profits from a business you operate overseas (sole trader, partnership, or via a foreign company you control) are generally assessable in Australia.

  • Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules may attribute undistributed foreign profits to you as an Australian resident shareholder
  • Transferring pricing rules apply to transactions between your Australian and overseas entities
  • GST generally doesn't apply to overseas services sold to overseas customers (GST-free supply)
  • ATO's Offshore Tax Avoidance regime (MAAL, DPT) targets large multinationals — less relevant for small business, but the framework exists

Foreign Pensions & Superannuation

Pension payments received from overseas schemes are generally assessable. The tax treatment depends on whether a tax treaty applies and whether contributions were taxed.

  • Some foreign pensions may be partially or fully exempt under a tax treaty (e.g. US Social Security, UK State Pension — rules differ)
  • Lump sums from foreign super funds: generally assessable, though the taxable component depends on when contributions were made
  • Transfers from a foreign super fund to an Australian super fund: the applicable fund earnings are generally assessable
  • Seek advice before transferring overseas super — the rules are highly scheme-specific

Foreign Income Tax Offset (FITO)

The FITO prevents double taxation — if you've paid tax on the same income overseas, you can offset that against your Australian tax bill.

AspectDetail
What qualifiesForeign income tax paid on income that is also assessable in Australia (withholding tax, income tax, profits tax)
How muchUp to the Australian tax payable on that foreign income — you can't use it to create a refund
CurrencyConvert foreign tax paid to AUD at the exchange rate on the date paid
Proof requiredForeign tax assessment notices, withholding tax certificates, or equivalent official documents
Carry forwardUnused offset (where the limit exceeds Australian tax) can be carried forward up to 5 years
Where to claimIndividual Tax Return (Item 20 — Foreign income); Business Activity Schedule if via a business
✅ Example You earned $20,000 dividend income from a US company. The US withheld 15% ($3,000) in withholding tax. In Australia, your marginal rate on this income is 37%. Australian tax on $20,000 = $7,400. You claim $3,000 FITO. Net Australian tax payable = $4,400. Total tax: $7,400 — no double taxation.

Australia has tax treaties with over 40 countries. Treaties can reduce or eliminate withholding tax on dividends, interest and royalties — and determine which country has the primary right to tax certain income.

Treaty Benefits — How to Access Them

📋 Generally, to claim treaty benefits:
  • Provide a Certificate of Australian Tax Residency (from the ATO) to the overseas payer
  • Complete the overseas country's withholding tax exemption or reduced rate form
  • Keep documentation of the treaty claim in your records for 5 years

Reporting Foreign Assets & Bank Accounts

Foreign Bank Accounts

Interest earned in foreign bank accounts must be declared in your Australian tax return. You do not need to report the account balance itself to the ATO, but you must declare the income earned.

Foreign Property

If you own real estate overseas:

  • 📋Declare any rental income (net of allowable expenses) in your tax return each year
  • 📋When you sell, a capital gain or loss arises — CGT applies as if it were an Australian asset (acquired after becoming a resident)
  • 📋The 50% CGT discount is available for properties held for 12+ months (for individual residents)
  • 📋Overseas property sold while you were a non-resident is generally not subject to Australian CGT

Foreign Shares & Investments

⚠️ Taxable Australian Property (TAP) vs Non-TAP When you leave Australia and become a foreign resident, you may make a deemed disposal of your foreign shares — triggering a CGT event — unless you elect to defer (and remain subject to Australian CGT on future gains). This is a significant decision requiring advice.

Common Scenarios for Small Business Owners

🛍️ Selling to Overseas Customers (Export)

Services or goods exported to overseas customers are generally GST-free. Your Australian business income is still assessable in Australia — you declare the AUD equivalent of all revenue.

💻 Receiving Payment via Overseas Platforms (Stripe, PayPal, Amazon)

Income is assessable when earned, not when transferred to your Australian account. Keep records of all transactions. Convert to AUD at the exchange rate on the date of each receipt, or use the RBA's monthly average rate with ATO approval.

👤 Employing Overseas Contractors

Payments to genuine overseas contractors for services performed outside Australia are generally deductible business expenses. Superannuation obligations do not apply to overseas contractors who are not Australian residents. Confirm the contractor's non-resident status in writing.

🏢 Paying for Overseas Software / Subscriptions

Digital services purchased from overseas suppliers for business use are deductible. Since 1 July 2017, overseas digital service providers with Australian revenue >$75,000 are required to register for and charge GST (Netflix Tax / Low Value Imported Goods rules) — so you may see GST charged on your business subscriptions.

Cross-Border Tax — Action Checklist

  • ☐Confirm your tax residency status at the start of each income year, especially if you've recently moved
  • ☐Track all foreign income throughout the year — employment, dividends, rent, business profits
  • ☐Record exchange rates on the date each payment is received, or use the ATO's published average annual rates
  • ☐Collect evidence of foreign tax paid — withholding tax certificates, foreign tax assessments, payment receipts
  • ☐Check if a tax treaty applies and whether it reduces your overseas withholding tax obligation
  • ☐Declare all foreign income in your Australian tax return (Item 20 and relevant schedules)
  • ☐Claim the FITO for any foreign income tax paid — don't pay tax twice
  • ☐Consider capital gains implications before selling foreign assets or changing your residency status
