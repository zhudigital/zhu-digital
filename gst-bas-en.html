GST & BAS:
What Every Small Business Must Know

GST (Goods and Services Tax) is a 10% tax on most sales in Australia. Once your turnover hits $75,000, registration is compulsory — and every registered business must lodge a Business Activity Statement (BAS). This guide explains what that means for you.

Do You Need to Register?

GST registration: mandatory vs optional

The $75,000 rule is simple — but there are a few situations worth knowing.

⚠️
Mandatory registration
Your GST turnover is at or above $75,000/year ($150,000 for non-profits). You must register within 21 days of reaching this threshold. Failure to do so means the ATO can back-calculate the GST you should have collected and make you pay it — even if you didn't charge it to clients.
✅
Voluntary registration
If you're under $75,000, you can still choose to register. Benefits include claiming GST credits on business purchases. Drawback: you must add GST to your invoices and lodge BAS regularly — adding compliance work.
🚗
Ride-share & taxi drivers
If you drive for Uber, DiDi, or any taxi/ride-share service, you must register for GST regardless of income — even if you earn under $75,000. This rule applies from your very first trip.
🌏
International sales
Most exports are GST-free. But if you sell digital products to Australian consumers through overseas platforms, GST rules may still apply. This area is complex — seek specific advice if relevant.
⚠️ Important: "GST turnover" means your gross income from taxable sales — not profit. Even if your business is losing money, you must register if your revenue crosses $75,000.

BAS — Business Activity Statement

What is BAS and how often do you lodge it?

BAS is your regular report to the ATO — it summarises your GST collected, GST credits claimed, and any PAYG withholding if you have employees.

📅
Quarterly lodgement (most common)
Q1: July–September → due 28 October
Q2: October–December → due 28 February
Q3: January–March → due 28 April
Q4: April–June → due 28 July

Most small businesses lodge quarterly.
📆
Monthly lodgement
Required if your annual GST turnover exceeds $20 million. Some businesses choose monthly voluntarily for better cash flow management. Due 21 days after the end of each month.
📝
Annual lodgement
Available to some small businesses (usually voluntarily registered for GST or those under certain thresholds). Due 31 October. You may still pay GST instalments quarterly even if you lodge annually.
💳
What goes in BAS
GST collected on your sales (1A)
GST credits on your purchases (1B)
PAYG withholding (if you have employees)
PAYG income tax instalments (if applicable)
Other taxes (wine equalisation, fuel tax credits)
✓ Good news: If GST credits (what you paid on purchases) exceed GST collected (what you charged customers), the ATO will refund you the difference. Keep all receipts and invoices.

How to Register & Lodge

Step-by-step: getting set up

Once registered, lodging BAS becomes a regular part of running your business.

1
Register for GST via the ATO
Log in to your myGov account linked to the ATO, or use the Business Portal. Select "Register for GST" under your ABN. You'll be asked your expected annual turnover and preferred BAS lodgement cycle. Most businesses choose quarterly.
→ ATO: Register for GST
2
Add GST to your invoices
From the date of registration, you must charge 10% GST on taxable sales. Your tax invoices must include: your ABN, "Tax Invoice" in the heading, the date, a description of goods/services, the GST amount (or a statement that the price includes GST), and your name/business name. Invoices over $1,000 must also show the purchaser's identity.
3
Keep records of all GST transactions
Save every tax invoice for purchases where you want to claim a GST credit. The ATO can audit you up to 5 years later. Good record-keeping software (Xero, MYOB, QuickBooks) automates most of this.
→ Read the record-keeping guide
4
Lodge your BAS on time
Lodge through myGov, the ATO Business Portal, or via your registered tax agent. Calculate: GST collected (1A) minus GST credits (1B) = amount owed to ATO (or refunded to you). Pay or claim the difference by the due date.
→ ATO: Business Activity Statements
5
Know the penalties for late lodgement
The ATO charges a Failure to Lodge (FTL) penalty: $330 per 28-day period (or part thereof), up to $1,650 for small entities. Interest also accrues on unpaid amounts. If you're struggling, call the ATO early — they may arrange a payment plan.
