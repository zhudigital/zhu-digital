Superannuation:
Your Obligations as an Employer

Super is compulsory in Australia. If you hire employees — or certain contractors — you must pay superannuation contributions on their behalf. Missing payments attracts the Superannuation Guarantee Charge, which costs significantly more than the original contribution.

Who Qualifies?

Which workers are entitled to super?

Most workers — including some contractors — are entitled to super from their employer. The old $450/month earnings threshold was removed from 1 July 2022.

✅
Full-time & part-time employees
All employees aged 18+ are entitled to super regardless of earnings. Employees under 18 qualify if they work more than 30 hours per week.
✅
Casual employees
Casuals also qualify — same rules apply. There is no longer a minimum monthly earnings threshold.
⚠️
Contractors (sometimes)
If a contractor is paid mainly for their labour, works under your direction, and uses your tools, they may legally be entitled to super — even with their own ABN. The ATO makes this determination based on actual work conditions.
❌
Not entitled
Contractors who run their own business, set their own hours, use their own equipment, and work for multiple clients generally do not qualify. Also: non-resident workers paid for work done overseas.

Payment Schedule

When do you pay super?

Super must be paid at least quarterly. Pay on time — late payments attract the Superannuation Guarantee Charge (SGC), which includes extra fees and interest.

⚠️ The SGC trap: If you miss the due date by even one day, the ATO may assess the Superannuation Guarantee Charge. SGC is calculated differently from ordinary super — it applies to total salary (not just ordinary time earnings), includes an interest component, and an admin fee. The total cost exceeds what you'd have paid on time. If you're behind, disclose and pay as soon as possible — the ATO has a voluntary disclosure option.
✓ Tip: Many employers pay super monthly (in line with payroll) rather than quarterly. This reduces the risk of missing payments and makes cash flow planning easier.

How to Pay Super

Step-by-step: paying super correctly

All super must be paid through SuperStream — the ATO's electronic payments system.

1
Get your employee's super fund details
When you hire someone, give them a Standard Choice Form within 28 days. They nominate their super fund. If they don't choose, you must use their stapled fund (the ATO tells you which one) or your default MySuper fund.
→ ATO: Stapled super funds
2
Calculate the super amount
Multiply ordinary time earnings by 11.5% (2026–27 rate). "Ordinary time earnings" typically means base pay, some allowances, and commissions — but not overtime. Use payroll software to automate this calculation.
3
Pay via SuperStream
You must send super electronically using SuperStream-compliant software. Most payroll platforms (Xero, MYOB, QuickBooks) are compliant. You'll need each fund's Unique Superannuation Identifier (USI) and your employee's member number.
→ ATO: SuperStream explained
4
Keep records for 5 years
Save all super payment records, choice forms, and fund details. The ATO can audit super payments going back 5 years. Good record-keeping protects you if any disputes arise.
